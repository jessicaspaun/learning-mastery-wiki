# Measure progress and mastery

[Wiki home](README.md) · [Design practice](practice-design.md) · [Codex coaching playbook](codex-coach.md) · [Advanced assessment and maintenance](mastery/assessment-and-maintenance.md)

## Check three outcomes

| Outcome | When to test | Example for guitar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current performance | During or directly after practice | Play the two bars after trying the new fingering or tempo |
| Retention | After a meaningful gap, before refreshing the target | Record the same phrase at the next session or a week later at a comparable tempo |
| Transfer | On a fresh, relevant variant or in the intended setting | Play the progression with a backing track, from another starting bar, or with another musician |

Decide what support is allowed. A programmer can use documentation in an authentic task. A soccer player can take a physical warm-up before a baseline. A retention check for a guitar passage can follow a general warm-up, before rehearsal of that passage. Keep support comparable across attempts, and log what you used. Choose the gap based on how long you need to retain the ability. [Learning versus performance](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000) · [Learning and transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6)

## Choose measures that capture your goal

| Domain | One measurable outcome | One quality check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factual knowledge | Correct answers out of total prompts after a delay | Explains a relationship and gives a valid example |
| Guitar | Clean, in-time changes out of opportunities at a specified tempo | Pulse and phrasing hold through one full take |
| Writing | Readers can accurately paraphrase the main claim or complete the intended action | A genre-specific rubric covers organization, clarity, and fit for the reader |
| Math | Correct solutions on a mixed set of unfamiliar problems | Chooses a valid method, explains it, and checks the result |
| Programming | Test results by category, including edge cases | Explains the code and independently diagnoses an example failure |
| Soccer | Appropriate decisions and successful execution per eligible opportunity | The player uses an available cue and adapts under changing pressure |

Measure the tradeoff when it matters. Pair speed with accuracy. For soccer, separate a sensible choice from pass completion. For prose, pair reader understanding with an appropriate quality rubric. Save raw examples such as drafts, recordings, test reports, or tagged clips so you can revisit how a score was produced.

## Calibrate confidence with attempts

Before a check, predict what you’ll get right or describe the likely failure. Compare the prediction to the result. If the work feels fluent and the score drops on a delayed, independent check, alter the next practice session. If a more demanding session feels difficult and the delayed result improves, keep the session design long enough to see whether the pattern repeats. [Bjork et al., self-regulated learning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143823) · [Deslauriers et al., classroom experiment on perceived learning](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31484770/)

## Copyable practice log

```text
Goal and standard:
Date and task:
Condition: baseline / practice / retention / transfer
Allowed tools and help:
Prediction:
Result or artifact:
One observed error or strength:
One change for the next attempt:
Next check date, task, and allowed support:
```

To make a milestone meaningful, choose the performance standard before testing. Check it on multiple days and across representative variations. As an example, a guitar learner might aim for two full recordings a few days apart at a chosen tempo with no extended breaks, then try playing with another person. The number of recordings and the threshold belong to the goal. Where observations are limited, record them as such and test again when feasible.
