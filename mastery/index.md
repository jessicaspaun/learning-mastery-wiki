# Mastering a new skill

[Wiki home](../README.md) · [Learning techniques](../techniques.md) · [Evidence and sources](../evidence.md)

Begin with a performance the learner cares about. Describe what a capable person does, under what conditions, with what tools, and how they recover when something changes. Then use smaller practice tasks that reveal what is limiting that performance. A learner who can repeat a demonstrated method may still need practice choosing it. A learner who can choose the method may need practice carrying it out when the constraints change. [Learning and transfer, National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6) · [Soderstrom and Bjork, learning versus performance](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000)

## A practice decision you can use today

1. **Sample the task.** Ask for one independent attempt with the tools the learner will normally use. Capture the work or observe specific behavior.
2. **Pick one explanation to test.** The issue may be recognition, strategy, execution, or the conditions of performance. Use a short comparison that would help tell them apart.
3. **Change one part of practice.** Supply a model for a missing procedure, compare examples when the learner chooses the wrong method, or add a live choice when a predictable drill has become easy.
4. **Check after a gap.** Compare a similar task before refreshing the answer or repeating a cue. Use a fresh case when item familiarity could account for success.
5. **Return to the goal.** Assess a useful version of the complete performance. If it holds and the skill will see infrequent use, schedule a later check that leaves time for a refresher.

The sequence is a practical synthesis. It can lead to very different exercises. A guitarist might target the beat during a chord change, then play the full chorus. A writer might learn to recognize why readers misidentify the main claim, then revise a new memo. A math learner might alternate problem types to practice selection. A programmer might debug an unfamiliar failure. A soccer player might rehearse receiving a pass with an opponent closing from a new angle. ## What the research leaves open

- **How long will mastery take?** Estimates depend on the standard, starting point, task, and amount of useful practice. Studies of elite performers also vary in how they define and measure training, who is included, and how much retrospective reports can establish. Sample the learner’s work and update the estimate as evidence accumulates. [Deliberate-practice evidence](expertise.md#what-deliberate-practice-research-can-tell-you) · [Estimating effort](self-directed-learning.md#estimate-the-effort-from-a-sample-of-the-work)
- **How much help and feedback is ideal?** Worked examples can benefit learners with low prior knowledge. The effect can reverse as learners become proficient. Feedback content, timing, and frequency are separate choices, with timing especially sensitive to task and setting. [Guidance and difficulty](expertise.md#adjust-guidance-and-difficulty-separately) · [Feedback decisions](feedback-and-plateaus.md#make-feedback-useful-for-the-next-attempt)
- **How broad will transfer be?** Strong evidence for one specific test supports claims about that test and related tasks. Changing cues, goals, or conditions creates a new question. Evidence for broad gains from training in another domain and for some pressure-training methods has important limits. [Transfer distance](transfer-and-pressure.md#name-what-has-to-carry-over) · [Rehearsing pressure](transfer-and-pressure.md#rehearse-a-relevant-kind-of-pressure)
- **How often should a skill be refreshed?** Decay varies with the task, measure, and practice history. The ideal interval for a person is a planning decision to update based on a relevant check. [Skill maintenance](assessment-and-maintenance.md#maintain-rarely-used-skills)

## Research scope

This expansion is a narrative review, checked November 14, 2026. Searches covered expertise development, practice conditions and feedback, curriculum design, transfer under changed conditions, assessment and decay, self-regulation, and AI coaching. Pages cite reviews, primary experiments, and frameworks with their relevant populations and outcomes. Studies with adult self-learners across these five domains are unevenly available. The exercises, worksheets, practice thresholds, and coaching scenarios apply relevant findings and can be tested locally. See [how the wiki interprets evidence](../evidence.md).
