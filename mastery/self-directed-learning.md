# Running your own learning program

[Mastery research](index.md) · [Wiki home](../README.md) · [Practice design](../practice-design.md)

In self-directed learning, you choose the next task, judge its result, and decide whether to change the plan. Models of self-regulated learning describe this as a cycle of planning, monitoring during performance, and reflecting afterward. A higher-education meta-analysis found benefits from extended training in these skills for academic performance, strategy use, and motivation. The studies taught packages of strategies, so the results give more confidence in the cycle as a whole than in any particular journal format or schedule. [Panadero, review of self-regulated learning models](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5408091/) · [Theobald, university training meta-analysis](https://www.uni-trier.de/fileadmin/fb1/prof/PSY/SUK/Theobald_2021-1.pdf)

## Use three types of goals

| Goal | Question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | What performance are you working toward? | Write project proposals that let a reviewer identify the decision, rationale, and next step. |
| Learning | What strategy or distinction do you need to discover or understand? | Learn two ways to structure a proposal and identify when each fits. |
| Process | What action can you try and observe this session? | Write two openings from the same notes and ask a reader to state the decision after each. |

When unfamiliar work stalls under a score target, try a learning goal that directs attention to useful strategies. Once you can use the strategy, use an outcome goal to check whether it serves the task. Studies comparing goal types include complex tasks and a small educational experiment in writing revision where shifting from process to outcome goals was useful. The best goal type can change within a single session. [Seijts and Latham, learning and performance goals](https://doi.org/10.5465/ame.2005.15841964) · [Zimmerman and Kitsantas, writing revision study](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.91.2.241)

## Estimate the effort from a sample of the work

Make a brief attempt and see how much you can do and how quickly you can respond to feedback. Estimate the next milestone after trying it. In two preregistered experiments, people often anticipated the general shape of improvement on a novel visuomotor task and misjudged their starting point and rate. Brief experience reduced errors in those predictions. A baseline gives you a better basis for planning in a new domain. If you need a time estimate, give yourself a range and update it after comparable sessions. [Zhang et al., experiments on predicting learning curves](https://actcompthink.org/pubs/ZhangEtAl_Cognition2025.pdf)

## Monitor a result you can use

Before practice, write one prediction about a specific outcome. After practice, record what happened and choose the next change. Include both **what you can do** and **how you practiced**. For example, “Solved two of four without the example. Both errors came from choosing a linear model for a proportional comparison. Next time, classify four mixed cases before solving.”

A meta-analysis of monitoring tools found a positive average effect on academic achievement across 32 interventions, with substantial variation. Tools covered journals, portfolios, and rubrics. Greater effects were associated with monitoring both learning content and behavior. Evidence from a broader goal-pursuit meta-analysis also supports prompting people to check progress. Use a log to make a decision or support a conversation with a coach. [Dignath et al., monitoring tools](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09718-4) · [Harkin et al., experimental progress monitoring](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/)

Confidence is easier to interpret when it refers to a future task you can check. Predict what you could do after a gap using the agreed tools. Collect an actual attempt. Recall studies found that predicting later recall after a delay was generally more accurate at identifying which items would be remembered than predicting straight after studying. For an essay or playing a song, use a dated sample and the relevant rubric. The recall evidence supports the idea of checking, while the skill assessment is an application. [Rhodes and Tauber, delayed judgments of learning](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21219059/) · [de Bruin et al., predictive cues for self-monitoring](https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.13267)

## Make starting and restarting easier

1. Name a recurring cue that you can recognize. “After I put Wednesday dinner away, I’ll put the guitar on my lap and record one take of the chorus.”
2. Set up the first action in advance. Keep the brief, sheet music, ball, or practice file easy to reach.
3. Choose a shorter version for a busy day. A programmer can predict the output of one function and run it. A writer can ask one reader a specific question.
4. When a session falls through, use the next available cue. Check whether time, equipment, difficulty, or lack of feedback made the plan hard to follow, then change that condition.

If–then planning research supports specifying a situation and an action across many goal domains. Habit-formation studies show large variation in the time needed to experience automaticity. These studies often examine health behaviors, so apply them most directly to the routine of starting a session. The ability being practiced still needs its own measures. [Gollwitzer and Sheeran, psychology of planning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-021524-110536) · [Lally et al., habit formation in daily life](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) · [Singh et al., habit time systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)

Motivation can come from enjoying the work and from valuing what it makes possible. A meta-analysis of student motivation found that intrinsic interest and personally valuing the activity were associated with different desirable outcomes, including success and persistence. Most effects in that synthesis were associations. In practice, choose a project with a real purpose or a partner you want to play with, and keep challenge within reach. When practice feels pointless, revisit the outcome before adding time. [Howard et al., student motivation meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620966789) · [National Academies, motivation to learn](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/8)

## Ask for a specific kind of help

| What you need | What to ask someone to do |
| --- | --- |
| A demonstration | Show a short task and name the cues or decisions. Then watch me try a comparable task. |
| Error diagnosis | Compare these two attempts and identify the first meaningful difference. |
| Calibration | Score my work with this same criterion before seeing my self-assessment. |
| Strategy practice | Let me teach the idea back, ask a question about an edge case, and check the explanation. |
| A path into real performance | Give me a small authentic task and one review. Then give me a fresh one with the usual tools. |

Teach-back involves retrieving and organizing knowledge. Meta-analyses found average benefits from preparing to teach and teaching. One identified gains in settings with opportunities for interaction, and a later analysis found that the expectation of teaching during initial study shaped the benefit. Concrete questions reveal where an explanation needs more work. For example, ask a learner to explain a sorting strategy, then have the listener supply a case with duplicate values. Confirm the result with a reference, test, or more experienced person. [Kobayashi, learning by teaching meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpr.12221) · [Kobayashi, teaching expectancy meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09871-4)

## Run a short weekly review

- **Practice delivered.** What did you actually attempt, with what support?
- **Capability gained.** What can you now do after a gap or on a fresh example?
- **Main obstacle.** Is it starting, using the right strategy, getting feedback, or applying the skill in context?
- **Next experiment.** What one change will you try? What observation would make you keep it?

This workflow is an application of the cited evidence. Keep only the notes that help you choose practice. If the plan is working and the learner is happy with the pace, the next useful action may be another session.
