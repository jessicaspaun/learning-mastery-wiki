# Transfer and performance under pressure

[Wiki home](../README.md) · [Mastery research](index.md) · [Assessment and maintenance](assessment-and-maintenance.md)

Pick a setting where the skill needs to work. A guitarist might need to resume a song when a bandmate misses a cue. A programmer might need to diagnose a fault in an unfamiliar repository. Both can practice the ingredients and then check whether they can recognize and use them in the setting that matters.

## Name what has to carry over

**Transfer** describes how earlier learning affects performance in a different task or context. Near and far describe degrees of similarity. A change in time, tools, social setting, task format, or knowledge domain can alter how demanding the test is. A delayed repetition of the same task primarily checks retention. A delayed attempt on a new task checks both retention and transfer. State what changed so the result tells you what the learner can handle. [Barnett and Ceci, transfer taxonomy](https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Barnett_Ceci_2002.pdf)

| Skill | Near transfer test | Further or more authentic test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guitar | Apply a practiced strumming pattern to a fresh progression at a similar tempo | Accompany a singer and adjust when their phrasing or count-in changes |
| Writing | Use the same evidence structure in a new memo for the same kind of reader | Adapt the material for a different audience that needs a different decision |
| Math | Solve a new problem with the same structure using different numbers | Recognize the structure in a graph, a word problem, or an unlabeled set alongside similar-looking counterexamples |
| Programming | Use a practiced testing technique on a new function | Identify a violated invariant in unfamiliar code, with normal tools and an incomplete bug report |
| Soccer | Use the same first-touch technique when a pass arrives from a new angle | Receive under live pressure and choose whether to pass, shield, turn, or let the ball run |

These are examples of increasing the demands on the specific ability. Broad claims about transfer depend on what researchers measure and how they design the comparison. A meta-analysis of programming instruction reported positive pooled estimates on some cognitive measures, with larger effects in studies using untreated comparison groups and in published studies. A study-quality analysis of music training in children found approximately zero average gains outside music after accounting for design quality. These findings concern different populations, outcomes, and interventions. A coaching plan can tie the promise of transfer to a particular task and test it directly. [Scherer et al., programming transfer](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000314) · [Sala and Gobet, music training and transfer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683441/)

## Teach the learner to recognize when a skill applies

One difficulty is spotting the relevant relationship in a new example. Gentner and colleagues found that learners who compared two cases illustrating a negotiation principle developed better accounts of the shared structure and used the principle more often in new tasks, including a face-to-face negotiation. Two school physics experiments found that learners who generated rules from contrasting cases before instruction were better able to apply the underlying ratio structure in a new topic. These are examples of transfer across cases with an identifiable shared structure. [Gentner et al., analogical encoding](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerLoewensteinThompson03.pdf) · [Schwartz et al., contrasting cases](https://aaalab.stanford.edu/assets/papers/2011/Practicing_versus_inventing.pdf)

1. **Compare two cases with a shared structure.** Put them side by side and have the learner identify which elements play the same role. Ask what evidence would make the method appropriate in both.
2. **Add a tempting counterexample.** Use a case with similar surface details and a different underlying relationship. Ask which condition breaks and what approach fits now.
3. **Remove the category label.** Give a new case and have the learner independently identify the relevant cue and choose a response.
4. **Offer a prompt after the first attempt if needed.** Record success before and after the prompt. Applying the method once the coach names it shows that execution is available and that recognizing when to use it needs practice.

For example, compare a code bug involving empty input with one involving an input that is exactly at a limit. Both call for examining boundaries, so identify the precondition, triggering input, and expected behavior in each. Then present a third case, a program that returns different answers for identical inputs. Ask the learner to test which evidence points to a boundary issue and which suggests state, ordering, or another cause. The exercise is an application of analogical comparison. Classic and later research also documents negative transfer when a familiar approach competes with a better one. [Barnett and Ceci, positive and negative transfer](https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Barnett_Ceci_2002.pdf) · [Bilalić et al., familiar solutions in chess](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17418112/)

When you want to assess adaptation, try a second version of the task. Give the learner a relevant new resource or a small amount of feedback, then check how effectively they use it on a further case. Unprompted performance and the ability to learn from new information answer different questions. A programmer might receive the documentation for an unfamiliar API and explain which assumption they need to revise before trying again. This follows the idea of *preparation for future learning*. [Bransford and Schwartz, rethinking transfer](https://doi.org/10.3102/0091732X024001061)

## Keep the cues and choices that matter

In a representative practice task, the learner can pick up the key information available in the target setting and respond in a way that matters there. Researchers comparing video and live soccer penalty tasks observed that goalkeepers allocated gaze differently when they actually moved to intercept the ball. The movement required in a task can change which cues the learner uses. A spoken prediction from a video can assess an aspect of recognition. An on-field attempt can assess whether the player uses the information to move in time. [Pinder et al., representative learning design](https://www.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/380698/Representative-Learning-Design-and-Functionality-of-Research-and-Practice-in-Sport.pdf) · [Dicks et al., goalkeeper gaze and task constraints](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/APP.72.3.706.pdf)

To design a compact simulation, list what you need to **notice**, what **choices** you have, what you must **do**, and what lets you **check the outcome**. Keep those features when you simplify the surrounding activity. For a first-touch decision, a two-against-one drill can preserve a moving opponent and a teammate while creating more attempts than a full match. For technical writing, ask a real reader to follow a short guide using sample data and identify the first point where they get stuck. For ensemble guitar, use a partner’s audible count-in so the player has to time their entrance to a human cue. These are practice design examples. The fidelity that matters depends on the intended performance. [Pinder et al., considering functionality and action in training](https://doi.org/10.1123/JSEP.33.1.146)

Opportunity also affects what shows up outside practice. A workplace training meta-analysis found that motivation and a supportive environment were associated with transfer, with some relationships stronger for open skills such as leadership than for structured software tasks. Measures from the same source and context tended to inflate the relationships. Ask whether the learner had a chance to use the skill and collect an artifact, an observation, or a test result where feasible. [Blume et al., workplace transfer meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)

## Rehearse a relevant kind of pressure

Pressure can change the way a learner uses attention. Experiments document several routes to performance failure, including attention captured by worries and step-by-step monitoring of a practiced action. The effects depend on the task and the kind of pressure. Compare a low-pressure attempt with a comparable attempt under one relevant demand. This helps distinguish a gap in a prerequisite from a gap in performance under observation, time limits, or consequences. [DeCaro et al., multiple mechanisms under pressure](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/8/1250/files/2018/07/Choking-Under-Pressure-Multiple-Routes-to-Skill-Failure-25g8myz.pdf) · [Beilock and Carr, putting under pressure](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-1304701.pdf)

A meta-analysis of 14 studies in sports and law enforcement found a moderate pooled advantage for simulated pressure training on pressured posttests after excluding an outlier. The evidence on how long the benefit lasts and how well it carries into real competition was sparse. A small study of 18 string players found fewer technical errors over three brief performances of the same excerpt for different audiences on one day. That design leaves the contribution of repeated playing and the durability of gains open. [Low et al., pressure training meta-analysis](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26305/3/PressureTrainingForPerformanceDomains.pdf) · [Candia et al., repeated stage exposure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10067860/)

Once a learner can perform the skill in ordinary practice, try adding one agreed demand. The following are practice examples drawn from the research principles. Ask how demanding the attempt felt and compare performance with the baseline.

| Domain | Small rehearsal | What to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guitar | Give one continuous take for a friend, using the planned count-in | Pulse, section entrances, and recovery after a missed chord |
| Writing | Give a draft to a new reader for a real decision, with the usual revision window | Whether they can find the decision and reasons before requesting clarification |
| Math | Try a short timed set only if time is part of the goal. Use comparable timed and untimed sets. | Method selection and accuracy, plus where time was spent |
| Programming | Pair with an observer in an interview rehearsal or handle one simulated incident in a sandbox | Whether the learner reproduces, explains, tests, and verifies before declaring a fix |
| Soccer | In a small game, add a score and a short remaining period | Whether the player scans, sees a viable option, and executes under match-like constraints |

If a task breaks down even during a calm attempt, practice the missing component. If it works in that setting and breaks down under pressure, lower the added demand enough to give the learner useful attempts. Rehearse how to reset. A guitarist might rejoin on the next downbeat, and a programmer might return to the last reproducible failing test. Reassess in the actual setting or a close simulation when possible. These are practical adaptations. The research leaves the ideal dose and progression open. [Low et al., findings and research gaps](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26305/3/PressureTrainingForPerformanceDomains.pdf)

## Separate pressure from interruptions and fatigue

Interruptions can disrupt the sequence of steps even when someone returns to the task quickly. In one lab experiment, very brief interruptions increased sequencing errors on a practiced multistep task. A different experiment found that people with time to prepare for an interruption resumed more quickly. For work that routinely gets interrupted, rehearse leaving a short note with the current state and next move. For example, “The empty-list test passes. Next, verify the equality case before editing the filter.” A restart cue is a practical application of the resumption research. [Altmann et al., momentary interruption experiment](https://interruptions.net/literature/Altmann-JExpPsycholGen14.pdf) · [Trafton et al., preparing to resume](https://gregtrafton.com/papers/preparing.to.resume.pdf)

Fatigue is a condition to record when comparing attempts. In soccer studies, tasks intended to induce mental fatigue have produced decrements on some decision and technical performance measures. Effects vary with the task and outcome, and reduced performance under fatigue answers a different question from whether that condition supports learning. If late-game execution matters, compare what happens early and late in ordinary practice or matches. A repeated problem across similar conditions is easier to interpret than a single late-session score. [Smith et al., soccer decision experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26949830/) · [Soccer technical performance meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.922630/full)

## Build a transfer check

1. **Choose a task the learner has yet to see.** Specify the context, what stayed similar, what changed, and which tools are allowed.
2. **Keep the first attempt visible.** Record when the learner recognized the cue, what they chose, and whether it worked. If they ask for help, note which part it helped them complete.
3. **Use a realistic outcome and a process measure.** A completed pass and a sensible choice are different observations. A passing test suite and a correct explanation of the failure are different observations too.
4. **Try a second equivalent case on another occasion.** Change a meaningful feature while keeping the standard stable. Record obstacles such as missing information, fatigue, and interruptions.
5. **Choose the next rehearsal from the result.** If the learner misses the cue, compare cases. If they see it and cannot execute, practice the action. If both work and the result is lost after a gap, plan a [retention check and refresher](assessment-and-maintenance.md).

This is a coaching procedure synthesized from transfer and measurement research. Its threshold should match the intended use. One successful attempt provides one observation. Multiple independently completed attempts across relevant cases give a better picture of how wide the skill’s range is. [Barnett and Ceci, transfer dimensions](https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Barnett_Ceci_2002.pdf) · [Blume et al., transfer measures](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
