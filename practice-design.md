# Design a practice plan

[Wiki home](README.md) · [Techniques](techniques.md) · [Measure progress](assessment.md) · [Design a full curriculum](mastery/curriculum.md) · [Diagnose a plateau](mastery/feedback-and-plateaus.md)

## Turn a broad goal into a task

Fill in “I want to **do this task**, under **these conditions**, to **this standard**.” Choose an artifact or behavior that would demonstrate it. For “learn programming,” a task could be “Given a new specification and access to language documentation, build a command-line tool that reads a file, handles invalid rows, and passes a short test suite.” For “learn to write,” choose one genre and name the reader and what the writing should accomplish. Define a baseline that is small enough to try within your first session.

The following workflow is a practical synthesis. Tailor the order and amount of practice to the goal and prior knowledge. [How People Learn II](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/7) · [Learning and transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6)

## Use the baseline to choose a next exercise

| What happens on the attempt | Next practice task |
| --- | --- |
| I can follow a model and forget it the next day | Reconstruct or perform it from memory after a delay, then check and correct |
| I have no idea how to start | Check a prerequisite, study a short model, then complete part of a similar task |
| I can say the rule and make the same application error | Work one example slowly, predict the result of a step, and compare the two outcomes |
| I can solve a page of one type and get lost when the types are mixed | Compare two confusable approaches, then choose a method on unlabeled tasks |
| My isolated technique is good and breaks down in the full performance | Practice the part in context with one real constraint, then add the remaining constraints |
| I can succeed with a hint and stall independently | Repeat a comparable task with one less hint, then schedule an independent retest |
| I get different opinions about quality | Choose an audience or performance standard and ask for observations tied to it |
| I keep practicing without seeing improvement | Compare dated attempts, identify a recurring error, and change one practice variable |

## Example of a 30-minute session

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 4 min | Make a cold attempt at something from an earlier session. Predict your result before you check it. |
| 6 min | Study a model, compare two cases, or get feedback on a prior attempt. Choose one thing to change. |
| 12 min | Do focused repetitions, solving, drafting, or building. After each useful attempt, check what changed. |
| 6 min | Put the practiced part into a whole task or try a small variation. Record the outcome. |
| 2 min | Log the main result and choose the next delayed check. |

This is a planning example. In soccer, do an appropriate physical warm-up before the first measured attempt. In writing, drafting and revising may take separate sessions. For a short study session, an attempt, a check, and a future review can be enough.

## Adjust difficulty one dimension at a time

- **Make it easier** by reducing the number of steps or choices, giving an example, lowering tempo, or allowing a reference. Keep the part of the task that reveals the target ability.
- **Make it harder** by removing a cue, changing the problem format, increasing speed, introducing an opponent, or increasing the quality standard. Choose a dimension needed in the real task.
- **Hold difficulty steady** while you investigate a recurring error. One change at a time helps you see which cue, explanation, or task constraint helps.
- **Schedule a check under stable conditions** if you feel stuck. Compare it to a prior attempt at similar difficulty. Challenge is useful when it leads to successful practice, informative errors, or better later results.

Keep a mix of tasks you can already do, tasks that stretch one capability, and occasional complete performances. The effective amount of guidance depends in part on prior knowledge. Practice conditions can also change how difficult practice feels relative to how much the learner retains. [Kalyuga, expertise reversal](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0) · [Learning versus performance](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000)

## Four-week starting plan

| Week | Main work | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Define success and collect a baseline. Learn prerequisites and study a model. | Complete a smaller version with the relevant help documented. |
| 2 | Repeat the target component across several sessions. Use clear feedback and fade cues. | Attempt a similar task before review. Identify where help is still needed. |
| 3 | Mix tasks that require choosing among approaches. Put components into a fuller task. | Try a representative variation with the usual tools and no coaching. |
| 4 | Repeat a comparable task and test a new case, setting, or audience. | Compare with the baseline and set the next practice goal from the evidence. |

Choose a time and place you can keep. “After Tuesday dinner, I’ll spend 20 minutes tracing and testing the next function” makes the next action concrete. Set up access to the tool, practice partner, or example in advance. Use a small meaningful project or piece to make the practice relevant. For retention, plan reviews after gaps and preserve a consistent assessment when comparing sessions. [Implementation intentions research](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/220271792/FULL_TEXT.PDF) · [How People Learn II, motivation](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/8) · [Spacing research](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda%20et%20al%202008_psychsci.pdf)

When a demonstration is hard to follow, pause it at a meaningful boundary and check what you understood before moving on. When planning a delayed check, leave time between sessions for sleep. Research on multimedia instruction supports meaningful segmentation, and sleep research describes how sleep contributes to forming and retaining memories. [Rey et al., multimedia segmentation meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4) · [Sleep and memory review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40875205/)

## Apply the plan to other goals

- **Learning a subject from a book or course.** After a section, reconstruct the main idea and explain how two claims relate. Check your explanation against the source, generate an example, and answer a new question later. Writing an explanation can help you learn the subject. Feedback on the text itself addresses writing craft. [Karpicke and Blunt, retrieval from science texts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) · [Graham et al., writing to learn](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320914744)
- **Learning a language.** Space recall of useful words and phrases. Rehearse producing them with different prompts, then use them in a conversation or written exchange that serves your goal. Assess recall and communication separately. [Kim and Webb, second-language spacing](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479)
- **Learning a hands-on procedure.** Observe a model, practice the sequence with appropriate feedback, and use a checklist where the real task calls for one. Test the full task and a realistic variation, such as different equipment or a different starting condition. [Learning and transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6) · [Smith and Scarf, practice spacing and skill](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5476736/)
