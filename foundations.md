# What makes learning stick?

[Wiki home](README.md) · [Techniques](techniques.md) · [Design practice](practice-design.md)

## Start with the kind of performance you need

| Learning target | What you need to be able to do | What to practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts and vocabulary | Recall a term, fact, symbol, or rule when needed | Answer questions from memory with corrective feedback and revisit after a gap |
| Concepts and mental models | Explain causes, connect ideas, and recognize examples | Explain relationships, compare examples and counterexamples, and predict outcomes |
| Procedures | Carry out steps and recover from errors | Work through a model, complete missing steps, then perform independently |
| Method selection | Recognize which strategy fits the situation | Mix similar-looking tasks, choose a method, and justify the choice |
| Movement and timing | Coordinate an action reliably under relevant conditions | Practice the movement, use observable feedback, and build toward the full activity |
| Perception and judgment | Notice cues and act on them in time | Practice with representative signals and choices, such as defenders and passing lanes |
| Creative and social performance | Produce something that works for a particular audience or collaborator | Study examples, make an artifact, get feedback from a relevant person, and revise |

Most goals combine several rows. A guitarist can know the chord, fret it cleanly, miss the beat when changing to it, or lose the song after a mistake. Each observation suggests a different practice task. Prior knowledge also matters. A learner who lacks the vocabulary or prerequisite move may benefit from a demonstration before attempting a full performance. [How People Learn II, knowledge and reasoning](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/7) · [Worked examples in mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1) · [Soccer skill acquisition review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.772201)

## Separate what you can do now from what you can do later

**Performance** is what happens on the current attempt. A warm-up, hints, a labeled problem set, or a fresh demonstration can improve that attempt. **Retention** is what you can still do after time has passed. **Transfer** is what carries to a different task, example, or situation. In a review of verbal and motor learning, Soderstrom and Bjork found conditions that improved performance during practice while producing weaker delayed learning, and conditions with the reverse pattern. [Learning versus performance](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/11/soderstorm_ra_learningvsperformance.pdf)

Choose three checks. For example, solve a linear equation now with a reference sheet, solve a similar equation next week before reviewing, then recognize when to use an equation in a new word problem. For professional programming, an authentic transfer task can allow documentation while requiring independent design, testing, and debugging. Define the tools and help allowed for each check. [Learning and transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6)

## Build transfer into the practice

Vary a feature that matters to the goal. Change the starting chord, reader, problem representation, data shape, or defender’s angle. Ask which parts of the solution stay valid when the example changes. Compare cases that share an underlying structure and cases that look similar but need different responses. Check transfer explicitly because evidence for recall or near transfer can leave performance in substantially different settings uncertain. [Learning and transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6) · [Retrieval practice and transfer meta-analysis](https://rickardlab.ucsd.edu/pdf/PR_2018.pdf)

## Describe mastery within a scope

Define what “mastery” would look like in the setting you care about. A learner might aim to reliably play three songs with a group, explain and apply introductory algebra, write clear technical instructions, implement and maintain a small app, or contribute as a recreational midfielder. Useful criteria include accuracy, consistency across days, independence, adaptability, and recovery when something goes wrong. Speed matters when the target requires it.

Targeted practice gives you a way to improve a specific weakness. In the expertise literature, Ericsson and colleagues used a narrower definition of *deliberate practice* tied to structured improvement activities in established domains. Researchers differ on which activities to count and how much variation in expertise practice explains. Hours quantify exposure. Use repeated demonstrations of performance to judge whether the goal has been met. [Ericsson et al., original framework](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363) · [Macnamara et al., cross-domain meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24986855/) · [Ericsson and Harwell, definition and reanalysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396)
